Case briefing

What you need to know

The angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference. The angle in a semicircle is 90°. Angles in the same segment are equal. Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add to 180°.

A tangent meets a radius at 90°. Two tangents from one point are equal. The angle between a tangent and a chord equals the angle in the alternate segment. The perpendicular from the centre to a chord bisects it. Two radii make an isosceles triangle.

  1. Mark every radius: two radii mean an isosceles triangle.
  2. Look for the shape the theorem needs: a 'bow tie', an 'arrowhead', a quadrilateral, a tangent.
  3. Write the angle and the theorem's reason, every time. Reasons earn marks.
  4. Use ordinary angle facts too: triangles 180°, straight lines 180°.

WORKED EXAMPLEA, B and C are on a circle with centre O. Angle OAB = 20°. Find angle ACB, giving reasons.

Solution

1OA = OB (radii), so triangle OAB is isosceles: angle OBA = 20°.
2Angle AOB = 180° − 20° − 20° = 140° (angles in a triangle add to 180°).
3The angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference.
4So angle ACB = 140° ÷ 2 = 70°.
✓ Angle ACB = 70°
