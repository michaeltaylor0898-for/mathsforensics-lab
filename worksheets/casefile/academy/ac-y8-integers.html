For parents: how to use this sheet

Read Learn it together first and talk through the worked examples. Then let your child try the questions on their own. Start at Developing; move up when they get most of a level right. Answers are at the back.

What to listen for

Ask: will the answer be positive or negative, and why? For HCF and LCM, ask your child to say which one is the biggest number that divides both (HCF) and which is the smallest number both go into (LCM). Mixing them up is the commonest slip.

Learn it

What you need to know

Adding and subtracting negatives: use a number line. Subtracting a negative is the same as adding: 3 − (−5) = 3 + 5.

Multiplying and dividing: same signs give a positive answer, different signs give a negative answer. −6 × −4 = 24, but 18 ÷ −3 = −6.

Every whole number can be written as a product of prime factors, such as 60 = 2² × 3 × 5. Use a factor tree.

The HCF (highest common factor) is the biggest number that divides both. The LCM (lowest common multiple) is the smallest number both divide into.

Index notation: 2³ means 2 × 2 × 2 = 8. √49 = 7 because 7² = 49, and ∛64 = 4 because 4³ = 64.

Solution

2−4 − (−5) is the same as −4 + 5.
3−4 + 5 = 1.
✓ 1

WORKED EXAMPLEWork out −6 × −4 and 18 ÷ −3

Solution

1−6 × −4: same signs, so positive. 6 × 4 = 24.
218 ÷ −3: different signs, so negative. 18 ÷ 3 = 6.
✓ 24 and −6

WORKED EXAMPLEWrite 60 as a product of prime factors.

Solution

1Split with a factor tree: 60 = 2 × 30, then 30 = 2 × 15, then 15 = 3 × 5.
2Collect the primes: 2 × 2 × 3 × 5.
3Use index notation: 2² × 3 × 5.
✓ 60 = 2² × 3 × 5

WORKED EXAMPLEFind the HCF and the LCM of 12 and 18.

Solution

112 = 2² × 3 and 18 = 2 × 3².
2HCF: the primes they share, the lowest power of each: 2 × 3 = 6.
3LCM: every prime, the highest power of each: 2² × 3² = 36.
✓ HCF = 6, LCM = 36

Mistakes to look out for

  • Subtracting a negative as if it were a positive: −5 − (−2) is −3, not −7.
  • Forgetting that (−3)² is positive 9. A negative number squared is always positive.
  • Mixing up HCF and LCM. The HCF is never bigger than the numbers; the LCM is never smaller.
  • Writing 2³ as 6 (2 × 3) instead of 8 (2 × 2 × 2).
