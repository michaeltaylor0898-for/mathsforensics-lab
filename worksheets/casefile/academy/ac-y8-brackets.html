What to listen for

Ask: have you multiplied every term inside the bracket? For factorising, ask: what's the biggest thing that goes into every term? Your child can always check a factorised answer by expanding it again.

Learn it

What you need to know

Expanding a bracket means multiplying every term inside it by the term outside: 3(x + 4) = 3x + 12.

Watch the signs: a negative number outside changes the sign of every term inside. −2(b − 4) = −2b + 8.

Factorising is the reverse: find the highest common factor (HCF) of every term, write it outside a bracket, and work out what goes inside.

Check any factorised answer by expanding it again: you should get back to where you started.

WORKED EXAMPLEExpand 3(x + 4)

Solution

13 × x = 3x.
23 × 4 = 12.
✓ 3x + 12

WORKED EXAMPLEExpand 2a(a − 5)

Solution

12a × a = 2a².
22a × −5 = −10a.
✓ 2a² − 10a

WORKED EXAMPLEExpand and simplify 3(2x + 1) + 2(x − 4)

Solution

1Expand each bracket: 6x + 3 + 2x − 8.
2Collect like terms: 6x + 2x = 8x and 3 − 8 = −5.
✓ 8x − 5

WORKED EXAMPLEFactorise fully 4y² + 10y

Solution

1The HCF of 4 and 10 is 2, and both terms contain y: the HCF is 2y.
24y² ÷ 2y = 2y and 10y ÷ 2y = 5.
3Check: 2y(2y + 5) = 4y² + 10y ✓
✓ 2y(2y + 5)

Mistakes to look out for

  • Multiplying only the first term in the bracket: 3(x + 4) written as 3x + 4.
  • Sign errors with a negative outside: −2(3b − 4) is −6b + 8, not −6b − 8.
  • Not factorising fully: 8x² − 12x = 2x(4x − 6) isn't finished, because 4x − 6 still has a common factor of 2.
  • Forgetting to divide every term when factorising. Expanding again catches this.
