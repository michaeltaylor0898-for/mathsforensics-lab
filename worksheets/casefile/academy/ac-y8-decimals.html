For parents: how to use this sheet

Read Learn it together first and talk through the worked examples. Then let your child try the questions on their own. Start at Developing; move up when they get most of a level right. Answers are at the back.

What to listen for

Ask: where does the decimal point go, and why? For multiplying, listen for counting decimal places. For dividing, listen for making the number you divide by into a whole number first.

Learn it

What you need to know

Adding and subtracting: line up the decimal points, one under the other. Fill any gaps with zeros so both numbers have the same number of decimal places.

Multiplying: ignore the decimal points and multiply as whole numbers. Then count the decimal places in the question: the answer has the same number.

Dividing by a decimal: multiply both numbers by 10 (or 100) until you're dividing by a whole number. The answer doesn't change.

WORKED EXAMPLEWork out 12.7 + 3.85

Solution

1Line up the points and fill the gap: 12.70 + 3.85.
2Add column by column, carrying as normal.
✓ 16.55

WORKED EXAMPLEWork out 8 − 2.36

Solution

1Write 8 as 8.00 so the points line up.
28.00 − 2.36, borrowing as normal.
✓ 5.64

WORKED EXAMPLEWork out 0.6 × 0.04

Solution

1Multiply without the points: 6 × 4 = 24.
2Count decimal places in the question: 1 + 2 = 3.
3Give the answer 3 decimal places: 0.024.
✓ 0.024

WORKED EXAMPLEWork out 7.2 ÷ 0.3

Solution

1Multiply both numbers by 10: 72 ÷ 3.
272 ÷ 3 = 24.
✓ 24

Mistakes to look out for

  • Not lining up the decimal points: 12.7 + 3.85 written as 4.12 or 16.92.
  • Subtracting the smaller digit from the bigger in each column instead of borrowing: 8 − 2.36 = 6.36.
  • Counting decimal places wrongly when multiplying: 0.6 × 0.04 = 0.24.
  • Dividing by a decimal without multiplying both numbers by 10.
