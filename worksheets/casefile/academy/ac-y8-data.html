Learn it

What you need to know

Categorical data are words or categories (eye colour). Discrete data are counted and can only take certain values (number of siblings, shoe size). Continuous data are measured and can take any value (height, time).

A population is everyone you want to know about; a sample is the group you actually ask. A sample is biased if it isn't representative, for example asking only one year group or only people at a gym.

Good survey questions are clear, give a time frame, have response boxes that don't overlap and cover every answer, and don't lead people towards an answer.

Grouped data use class intervals such as 150 ≤ h < 160: from 150 up to, but not including, 160. A two-way table shows two kinds of information at once.

WORKED EXAMPLEIs each type of data categorical, discrete or continuous? Eye colour, number of siblings, height.

Solution

1Eye colour is described in words: categorical.
2Siblings are counted, and there are no halves: discrete.
3Height is measured and can be any value: continuous.
✓ Categorical, discrete, continuous

WORKED EXAMPLEImprove this survey question: How much TV do you watch? A lot / Some / A little

Solution

1'A lot' means different things to different people, and there's no time frame.
2Give a time frame and number boxes that don't overlap and cover every answer.
✓ How many hours of TV did you watch yesterday? 0 to 1 · more than 1 up to 2 · more than 2 up to 3 · more than 3

WORKED EXAMPLE30 students were asked how they travel to school. Complete the two-way table.

WalkBusTotal
Boys814
Girls7
Total30

Solution

1Boys who take the bus: 14 − 8 = 6.
2Girls in total: 30 − 14 = 16. Girls who walk: 16 − 7 = 9.
3Totals: walk 8 + 9 = 17, bus 6 + 7 = 13. Check: 17 + 13 = 30 ✓
✓ Boys bus 6; girls walk 9, total 16; walk 17, bus 13

Mistakes to look out for

  • Calling counted data continuous: anything you count (people, goals, shoe sizes) is discrete.
  • Response boxes that overlap (0–5, 5–10) or leave gaps, or no box for large answers.
  • Putting a boundary value in the wrong class: 160 belongs in 160 ≤ h < 170.
  • Saying a sample is biased 'because it's small'. Small samples are unreliable; biased samples leave groups out.
