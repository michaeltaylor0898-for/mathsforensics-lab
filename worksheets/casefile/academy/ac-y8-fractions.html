For parents: how to use this sheet

Read Learn it together first and talk through the worked examples. Then let your child try the questions on their own. Start at Developing; move up when they get most of a level right. Answers are at the back.

What to listen for

Ask: can you add these straight away, or do the bottom numbers need to match first? For dividing, listen for flip the second fraction and multiply. Mixed numbers should become top-heavy fractions before multiplying or dividing.

Learn it

What you need to know

The bottom number (denominator) says how many equal parts; the top number (numerator) says how many of them you have.

Adding and subtracting: the denominators must be the same. Find a common denominator, change both fractions, then add or subtract the numerators only.

Multiplying: multiply the tops and multiply the bottoms. Dividing: flip the second fraction and multiply.

Mixed numbers such as 212 become top-heavy fractions first: 52. Always simplify your answer.

WORKED EXAMPLEWork out 23 + 14

Solution

1Common denominator 12: 23 = 812 and 14 = 312.
2Add the tops: 812 + 312 = 1112.
✓ 1112

WORKED EXAMPLEWork out 34 × 25

Solution

1Tops: 3 × 2 = 6. Bottoms: 4 × 5 = 20.
2620 simplifies to 310 (÷ 2).
✓ 310

WORKED EXAMPLEWork out 56 ÷ 23

Solution

1Flip the second fraction and multiply: 56 × 32.
2= 1512 = 54 = 114.

WORKED EXAMPLEWork out 212 + 123

Solution

1Make them top-heavy: 52 + 53.
2Common denominator 6: 156 + 106 = 256.
3256 = 416.
✓ 416

Mistakes to look out for

  • Adding the denominators: 13 + 25 = 38. The denominators must match, and then only the tops are added.
  • Multiplying or dividing mixed numbers without making them top-heavy first.
  • Flipping the first fraction instead of the second when dividing.
  • Not simplifying the final answer, or leaving a top-heavy answer when a mixed number is asked for.
