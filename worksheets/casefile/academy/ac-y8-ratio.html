What to listen for

Ask: how many parts are there altogether, and how much is one part worth? Almost every ratio question comes down to finding one part. Watch out for dividing by each number in the ratio instead of by the total number of parts.

Learn it

What you need to know

A ratio compares amounts. 3 : 5 means for every 3 of the first, there are 5 of the second: 8 parts altogether.

Simplify a ratio by dividing every number by the same thing, like simplifying a fraction. The amounts must be in the same units first.

Sharing in a ratio: add the numbers to find the total parts, divide to find one part, then multiply.

  1. Add the numbers in the ratio: the total number of parts.
  2. Divide the amount by the number of parts: the value of one part.
  3. Multiply one part by each number in the ratio.
  4. Check: the shares add up to the amount you started with.

Solution

212 ÷ 6 = 2 and 18 ÷ 6 = 3.

Solution

13 + 5 = 8 parts.
2£40 ÷ 8 = £5 for one part.
33 × £5 = £15 and 5 × £5 = £25.
4Check: £15 + £25 = £40 ✓

WORKED EXAMPLERed and blue beads are in the ratio 2 : 7. There are 14 blue beads. How many red beads are there?

Solution

1Blue is 7 parts, and 7 parts = 14 beads.
2One part = 14 ÷ 7 = 2 beads.
3Red is 2 parts: 2 × 2 = 4.
✓ 4 red beads

Mistakes to look out for

  • Dividing the amount by each number in the ratio, instead of by the total number of parts.
  • Simplifying before changing to the same units (50 cm : 2 m is not 25 : 1).
  • Saying 3 : 5 means 35 are red. It's 38: there are 8 parts altogether.
  • Giving the shares in the wrong order.
