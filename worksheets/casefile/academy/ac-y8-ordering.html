What to listen for

Ask: can you make them all the same type first? The most common mistake is thinking a decimal with more digits is bigger (0.125 is smaller than 0.5). Writing the decimals with the same number of digits helps.

Learn it

What you need to know

To compare decimals, line up the decimal points and give them the same number of decimal places by adding zeros. Then compare digit by digit from the left.

To compare fractions, either change them to decimals (divide the top by the bottom) or give them a common denominator.

Useful facts: 12 = 0.5, 14 = 0.25, 34 = 0.75, 15 = 0.2, 18 = 0.125, 13 = 0.333…

< means is less than; > means is greater than. For negative numbers, the further below zero, the smaller the number.

WORKED EXAMPLEPut in order, smallest first: 0.4, 0.35, 0.405, 0.04

Solution

1Give them all 3 decimal places: 0.400, 0.350, 0.405, 0.040.
2Now compare them like whole numbers: 40, 350, 400, 405.
✓ 0.04, 0.35, 0.4, 0.405

WORKED EXAMPLEPut in order, smallest first: 35, 0.58, 23, 62%

Solution

1Change them all to decimals: 0.6, 0.58, 0.666…, 0.62.
2Compare: 0.58 < 0.6 < 0.62 < 0.666…
3Write them in their original form.
✓ 0.58, 35, 62%, 23

WORKED EXAMPLEWhich is bigger, 58 or 712?

Solution

1Common denominator 24: 58 = 1524 and 712 = 1424.
215 > 14.
✓ 58 is bigger

Mistakes to look out for

  • Thinking more digits means bigger: 0.405 is less than 0.45.
  • Comparing fractions by their denominators only, so 14 looks bigger than 13.
  • Ordering negative numbers as if they were positive: −0.3 is smaller than −0.03.
  • Ordering largest first when the question asks for smallest first.
