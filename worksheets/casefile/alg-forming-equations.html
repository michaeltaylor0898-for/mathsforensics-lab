Case briefing

What you need to know

An equation is a balance: do the same to both sides and it stays true. To form an equation, give the unknown a letter, write two expressions for the same thing, and set them equal.

Brackets first, fractions next (multiply every term by the denominator), then collect the letters on the side with more of them.

  1. Define your letter: what does x stand for?
  2. Write an expression for each fact, then make them equal.
  3. Expand brackets, clear fractions, collect like terms, solve.
  4. Answer the question that was asked, with units, and check it.

WORKED EXAMPLEA rectangle's length is 3 cm more than twice its width. Its perimeter is 42 cm. Find its area.

Solution

1Let the width be w cm. Then the length is 2w + 3.
2Perimeter: 2(w + 2w + 3) = 42, so 6w + 6 = 42.
36w = 36, so w = 6. The length is 15 cm.
4Area = 6 × 15 = 90 cm². Check: 2(6 + 15) = 42 ✓
✓ 90 cm²

Case closed: Forming and solving equations

  • Answer the question asked, not just the value of x.
  • Recognise when a problem gives a quadratic, and reject impossible answers.
  • Write a short algebraic proof.
