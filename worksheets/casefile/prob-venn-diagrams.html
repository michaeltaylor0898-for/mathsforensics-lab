Case briefing

What you need to know

ξ is everything. A ∩ B (intersection) is in both. A ∪ B (union) is in either or both. A′ is everything not in A.

Fill a Venn diagram from the middle outwards: the overlap first, then 'only A' and 'only B', then outside. For given that questions, the total becomes only the part you're told about.

  1. Put the overlap in first.
  2. Subtract to find 'only A' and 'only B'. Don't count the overlap twice.
  3. Check every region adds up to the total in ξ.
  4. For P(B given A), divide by the number in A, not the total.

WORKED EXAMPLE40 officers were asked if they have a dog (D) or a cat (C). 18 have a dog, 15 a cat, and 6 have both. Find the probability that an officer chosen at random has neither.

Solution

1Both: 6 in the overlap.
2Dog only: 18 − 6 = 12. Cat only: 15 − 6 = 9.
3Inside the circles: 12 + 6 + 9 = 27. Outside: 40 − 27 = 13.
4P(neither) = 1340. Check: 12 + 6 + 9 + 13 = 40 ✓
✓ 1340
